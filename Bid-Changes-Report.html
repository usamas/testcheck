High Med Ltd (lead) and Dr Singh Hammond Road (key party) are not the incumbent provider. This response sets out how we will transfer and retain the Sudbury Surgery workforce, meet every legal and contractual obligation, and protect uninterrupted care for 7,788 registered patients through the change of provider on 1 November 2026. Dr Usama Safeer is our named Mobilisation Lead, accountable to Clinical Director Dr Shumaila Mahmood and supported by Practice Manager Ms Manjot Kaur. The accompanying TUPE Mobilisation Plan attachment provides the milestone timeline and risk register. 1. Continuity of employment for transferring staff Document 4 (Lot 4, unredacted) identifies 14 staff who transfer under TUPE 2006 - a Lead GP, one Healthcare Assistant, two phlebotomists, the Practice Manager, a Deputy Practice Manager and seven reception/administrative staff - alongside three current vacancies (two GP posts at 37.5 and 20 hours, one Practice Nurse at 37.5 hours), giving 17 positions in total. We accept that all eligible employees transfer on their existing terms and conditions, with continuity of service preserved. We will issue Employee Liability Information requests and section 4A statements through the outgoing provider (NHSolutions), run a formal measures consultation, and confirm each individual's terms in writing before transfer. The majority of staff hold NHS Pension membership; we are an NHS Pension employing authority and will onboard members without a break, protecting accrued benefits and continuing employer contributions. Non-Agenda-for-Change terms for non-medical staff and non-standard medical terms are honoured as transferred. 2. Meeting legal and contractual obligations during transition We will discharge all TUPE duties: early engagement with NHSolutions, exchange of Employee Liability Information at least 28 days before transfer, election or establishment of appropriate representatives, and meaningful consultation on any proposed measures. Where the Practice Manager post is subject to an ongoing disciplinary process, the live process and all attaching rights transfer with the individual; we will take legal advice, continue the process fairly under transferred policies, and maintain interim operational governance through the Deputy Practice Manager under the direct oversight of Practice Manager Ms Manjot Kaur and Clinical Director Dr Shumaila Mahmood, so accountability is never left vacant. Data protection obligations are met under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018: High Med Ltd holds current ICO registration (certificate enclosed in Supporting Evidence), will complete the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and will execute a data processing/sharing agreement with the ICB and outgoing provider before any patient-identifiable data moves. 3. Patient identifiable data and clinical record migration A controlled migration of patient-identifiable data (PID) is the single highest transition risk and is owned by Caldicott Guardian and IG Lead Dr Muhammad Adem. We will agree the clinical system position with NHSolutions and the ICB at the first mobilisation meeting (the practice's current system - EMIS or SystmOne - is confirmed Day-1; the consortium operates both). Where systems match, records transfer in situ via GP2GP and the national deduction/registration process; where they differ we will use the approved clinical system migration route with full reconciliation. Lloyd George paper records will be audited, tracked and digitised where not already scanned, with chain-of-custody logging. A Data Protection Impact Assessment is completed before go-live, records are processed on the London Care Record and NHS infrastructure only, and any incident is reportable to the ICO within statutory timescales. No patient data is processed outside approved NHS systems. 4. Mobilisation plan: timeline, milestones and resources Mobilisation runs August to November 2026 under Dr Usama Safeer, governed by a weekly mobilisation board and a RAG-rated milestone tracker (see attachment). Key milestones: August - mobilisation board stood up, NHSolutions handover and Day-1 operational audit commissioned, ELI exchanged, ICB and PCN contacts confirmed; September - staff consultation, contracts of employment and pension onboarding, IT and data migration design, premises and CHP lease assignment progressed, DSPT and DPIA underway; October - system build and template configuration, data migration dry run and reconciliation, staff induction and training, supplies and telephony/booking continuity tested, BCP rehearsal; 1 November - go-live with hypercare. Each workstream (workforce, IT/data, premises, governance, clinical, communications) has a named owner, defined deliverables and exit criteria before go-live. 5. Business continuity during the TUPE transition Continuity of care is protected by a transition Business Continuity Plan covering the highest-risk patient pathways. Pathology and phlebotomy continue uninterrupted (two phlebotomists transfer); urgent and two-week-wait referrals, blood testing, cervical and other screening, childhood and seasonal immunisations, high-risk medication monitoring and palliative/end-of-life care are mapped with named responsible clinicians and fail-safe checks so nothing is dropped across the changeover. We will hold pre-agreed locum cover for the three vacant posts and mutual-aid arrangements with Brent Central K&W PCN practices (Tudor House, Preston Road, Ellis and Chalkhill). Telephony and online booking continuity is tested before go-live, and a downtime/disaster-recovery procedure protects access if IT migration is delayed. A hypercare period in the first four weeks gives daily monitoring of access, prescribing and referral safety. 6. Financial impact analysis All TUPE costs are reflected in our Commercial Schedule (Document 5D): salaries on transferred terms, employer NHS Pension and on-costs, locum cover for the three vacancies until substantive recruitment, redundancy/protected liabilities, and mobilisation costs (IT migration, record digitisation, induction and training). We have modelled affordability against the Lot threshold with contingency for the vacancy and disciplinary risks, and the bid remains within threshold across all years. The consortium's audited accounts and Financial Ratio Templates (Document 5C) evidence the financial resilience to absorb transition costs without destabilising service delivery. 7. Stakeholder engagement A communications and engagement plan, owned jointly by Dr Safeer and Ms Kaur, runs throughout mobilisation. Staff receive early, honest engagement through consultation meetings, one-to-ones and a written Q&A to reduce uncertainty and protect morale. Patients and the Patient Participation Group are informed through practice notices, the website, SMS and waiting-room materials in the community languages reflected in the list (over half of registered patients are of Asian heritage and around one in three do not use English as a main language), with reassurance that registration, appointments and repeat prescriptions continue unchanged. We will engage the ICB mobilisation contacts, Brent Central K&W PCN, local NHS trusts (London North West University Healthcare), community pharmacy and the voluntary sector, and align with Brent Health Matters outreach so seldom-heard groups are not left behind during the change. 8. Workforce retention and support measures Retaining the experienced Sudbury team is central to safe continuity. Our Staff Retention Policy (attached to Q3.2) sets out: protected terms and NHS Pension continuity; a structured 12-week induction and buddying; clinical and managerial supervision; funded CPD and appraisal; flexible-working and wellbeing support including Employee Assistance and Freedom to Speak Up access; and payment of at least the London Living Wage. We will hold individual retention conversations during consultation, address pension and role concerns directly, and target retention of transferring staff above 90 per cent at six months. High Med Ltd's track record of TUPE transfers and staff engagement evidences the effectiveness of these measures. 9. Risk identification and mitigation The TUPE Mobilisation Plan attachment carries a RAG-rated risk register with named owners, mitigations and escalation routes. The principal risks are: PID/record migration error (mitigated by DPIA, dry-run reconciliation and GP2GP/approved migration); the three clinical vacancies (active recruitment plus pre-agreed locum and PCN mutual-aid cover); the Practice Manager disciplinary matter (interim DPM governance under CD oversight, legal advice, fair continuation); staff anxiety and attrition (early engagement and retention package); premises/CHP lease assignment (early landlord engagement, lease secured to November 2032); and IT downtime (tested DR and telephony continuity). Each risk is reviewed at the weekly mobilisation board with clear exit criteria that must be met before go-live. 10. Risk monitoring and escalation discipline Risks are not only identified but actively monitored to closure. Every risk on the register carries a named owner, a RAG score, a defined mitigation and a specific escalation route, and is reviewed at the weekly mobilisation board where the trend (improving, static, worsening) and trigger points for activating contingency are recorded. Red and amber risks are escalated to the Clinical Director and, where they affect patient safety or data, to the ICB, within agreed timescales. Critical success factors - safe data migration, continuity of urgent pathways, sufficient clinical cover and information-governance assurance - must each meet defined exit criteria before go-live is authorised, and the board has the authority to delay any non-critical change to protect safety. This monitored, escalation-disciplined approach directly addresses the requirement for a risk register that lives and is acted upon, not one that is written once. 11. Relevant Authority resources and dependencies We identify clearly what we require from the Relevant Authority and partners so mobilisation dependencies are managed transparently. From the ICB and its mobilisation team we will need timely confirmation of the clinical-system position and licences, support for data-sharing and information-governance approvals, the registered-list and deduction data, the affordability and contract documentation, and a named ICB mobilisation contact for issue resolution. From the outgoing provider (NHSolutions) we require Employee Liability Information, the Day-1 operational baseline, asset and compliance records, and a co-operative handover. From CHP we require lease assignment and premises-compliance certificates; from NHS England, GP2GP and national registration support; and from the PCN, Enhanced Access and mutual-aid agreement. Each dependency is logged with an owner, a required-by date and an escalation route, so any delay on the Authority's side is surfaced early and jointly managed rather than allowed to threaten go-live. 12. Relevant experience and evidence of effectiveness Our approach is evidenced, not theoretical. The consortium has managed TUPE transfers and managed-practice transitions while maintaining safe, continuous care and a CQC 'Good' rating at Dr Singh Hammond Road. From this experience we apply specific, proven lessons: exchange Employee Liability Information and begin individual consultation early; protect terms, pensions and continuity of service to maintain trust; rehearse data migration with full reconciliation before cutover; map every urgent clinical pathway to a named owner; and provide visible leadership presence and honest communication throughout. These measures have previously delivered high transferring-staff retention and uninterrupted patient care, and they underpin our target of retaining over 90% of transferring staff at six months. The attached TUPE Mobilisation Plan operationalises each lesson into milestones, owners and exit criteria. In summary, the consortium provides a complete, legally compliant and well-resourced TUPE and retention approach that protects every transferring employee, secures patient-identifiable data in line with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, manages every risk and Authority dependency to defined exit criteria, and guarantees uninterrupted, safe care through go-live on 1 November 2026.
